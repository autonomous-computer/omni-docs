title: "Webhooks"
description: "Create signed webhook endpoints in the TUROS dashboard, verify deliveries, and replay what your receiver missed."

Webhooks push events to a receiver you host - most commonly `monitor.match` events from a saved [monitor](/product/monitors). Every delivery is signed so your receiver can verify it came from TUROS.

## Configure endpoints in the dashboard

Create endpoint destinations, change event subscriptions, rotate signing secrets, send test events, and replay deliveries from the webhooks section of the [TUROS dashboard](https://turos.app). These actions require your signed-in organization session. Don't give an API key or an autonomous agent authority to make them.

To wire up a monitor:

1. Go to the webhooks section and create an endpoint with your receiver's URL.
2. Subscribe the endpoint to `monitor.match`.
3. Save the signing secret - TUROS shows it once.
4. Point the saved monitor's delivery at the endpoint.
5. Send a test event and confirm your receiver verifies and accepts it.

## Verify every delivery

Your receiver must verify the `x-secapi-signature` header against the exact raw request body before parsing JSON, and reject deliveries with stale timestamps or invalid signatures. Delivery is at-least-once, so store the delivery or event identifier in a durable idempotency table and skip work you've already done.

Process deliveries in this order:

1. Read the raw body and verify `x-secapi-signature`.
2. Reject old timestamps and invalid signatures.
3. Record the delivery identifier for idempotency.
4. Queue the work and return a fast `2xx`, then retrieve the filing evidence you need downstream.

Keep the event type, event ID, request ID, monitor ID, accession number, and filing URL with the queued work. An event payload can be incomplete or retried - it isn't a substitute for the filing source.

## Replay a missed delivery

If your receiver was down or rejected a delivery, replay it from the delivery history in the dashboard. Replay re-emits the stored event to the endpoint, so your idempotency table is what prevents double-processing. Automatic retry and endpoint auto-pause are being rolled out; until they reach your organization, treat replay as a manual step.

## Rotate a signing secret

Rotate the endpoint's secret from the dashboard when a secret may have been exposed or on your normal rotation schedule. Update the receiver with the new secret before deliveries resume, or verification will fail.

## Prefer polling? Use streams

If your worker needs explicit checkpoints and controlled replay instead of a public receiver, poll stream events with a cursor and commit the cursor only after a durable write. See the stream routes in the [Delivery API reference](/api-reference/delivery).
