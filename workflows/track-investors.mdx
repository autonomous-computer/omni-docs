Follow investment managers and activists through reported holdings, position changes, beneficial-ownership disclosures, and the ideas they explain in fund letters.

## Available now: holdings and ownership disclosures

1. Find a manager or issuer with the institutional-owner endpoints.
2. Retrieve 13F filings and normalized holdings for the reporting periods you need.
3. Use `POST /v1/owners/13f/compare` to identify additions, exits, and position changes.
4. Add 13D and 13G disclosures when an investor crosses a beneficial-ownership threshold or reports activist intent.
5. Keep the filing accession, reported period, filed date, issuer identity, and manager identity with every comparison.

13F data is delayed and reports long U.S. holdings within its regulatory scope. It is not a live portfolio, complete exposure map, or proof of an investor's current thesis.

## Fund Letters

Fund Letters adds cited manager commentary, theses, company mentions, and changes in stated views to the same investor workflow. The endpoints are live on the production API.

1. Find the manager with `GET /v1/fund-letters/managers`, or go straight to `GET /v1/fund-letters/managers/{manager_id}/overview` for a one-screen briefing.
2. List their letters with `GET /v1/fund-letters`, or search across bodies with `GET /v1/fund-letters/search`.
3. Pull the structured theses with `GET /v1/fund-letters/theses` and compare two periods with `GET /v1/fund-letters/diff`.
4. Keep the letter id, period, and page anchor with every observation, and verify quotes against `/document?format=markdown`.

Use the [Fund Letters capability guide](/intelligence/letters) for the coverage boundary, permitted documents, source and distribution rules, and metering. Documented coverage begins with 2025 Q1 — do not treat letters as a comprehensive historical archive.

## A combined investor record

The useful comparison is not “what did the filing say?” or “what did the letter say?” in isolation. It is:

- what the manager reported owning;
- how that position changed between reporting periods;
- what the manager publicly said about the company or theme;
- when the reported holding and stated thesis diverged; and
- which filing or permitted document supports each observation.

Start with SEC ownership data in the free sandbox, then layer Fund Letters on top for the stated rationale behind a position.
